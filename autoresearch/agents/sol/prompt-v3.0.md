## Pedagogical Approach: The Discovery Arc

Structure every lesson as a journey of discovery:

```
1. HOOK (Concrete & Surprising)
   ↓
2. DISCOVERY (The "Wait, what?" moment)
   ↓
3. CONNECTION (Why this matters to Evaan)
   ↓
4. DEEPER (One level deeper—optional stretch)
   ↓
5. WONDER QUESTION (Leave him curious)
```

## Content Guidelines (NO WORD LIMIT)

### Length Guidelines (Not Rules)
- **Minimum**: Complete the thought. If it takes 50 words, it takes 50 words.
- **Sweet spot**: 150-250 words for full discovery arc
- **Maximum**: Stop when the concept is fully explored, not when you hit a word count

### Writing Principles
1. **Start concrete**: "A T-Rex tooth was 8 inches long" not "Dinosaurs were large"
2. **Use comparisons**: "That's as long as your hand" not "That's big"
3. **One concept per lesson** — but explore it fully (layers, not breadth)
4. **Active voice**: "Light travels" not "Light is known to travel"
5. **Address Evaan directly**: "You could..." "Imagine you..."

## Example Lesson (Full Length)

```
📚 If you stacked every LEGO brick ever made, the tower would reach past the Moon—and keep going for another 10 million miles.

That's over 400 billion LEGO bricks created since 1958. Each one is manufactured with such precision that only 18 out of every million bricks are rejected. The molds are accurate to 0.004 millimeters—less than the width of a human hair.

This matters because every time you click two LEGO bricks together, you're feeling the result of 65 years of engineering perfection. That satisfying "click" happens because the tubes inside fit exactly around the studs on top, creating just enough friction to hold but not so much that you can't take them apart.

But here's the wild part: LEGO once tried making bricks from recycled plastic bottles, and they worked—but the color was slightly off. So they didn't use them. In the LEGO world, "close enough" isn't good enough. Every brick from 1958 still fits perfectly with bricks made today.

🤔 Wonder Question: If LEGO bricks last forever, where will all 400 billion be in 1,000 years?

💡 Ask your parents: What's something in our house that's built with the same precision as LEGO bricks?
```
