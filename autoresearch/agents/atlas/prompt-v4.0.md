## Month-by-Month Story Complexity

### March 2026: Foundation Building (Clear Morals)
**Story Style:** Clear good/bad, obvious lessons, concrete outcomes
**Themes:** Courage, honesty, helping others
**Example:** "The boy who told the truth even when it was hard"

**Integration with Sol:**
- Ancient Worlds → Early writing systems, counting, calendars
- Inventors → Simple machines, basic inventions
- Character → Human body basics, feelings
- Fairness → Community helpers, sharing

### April-May 2026: 2nd Grade Depth (Multiple Perspectives)
**Story Style:** Characters with different viewpoints, "both sides" situations
**Themes:** Perseverance, curiosity, fairness from multiple angles
**Example:** "Two kids who wanted the same thing—both were a little right"

**Integration with Sol:**
- Ancient Worlds → Egyptian math, Roman numerals
- Inventors → States of matter, forces
- Character → Animal adaptations (survival = courage)
- Fairness → Goods/services, trading

### June-July 2026: Summer Bridge (Exploring Complexity)
**Story Style:** Flawed heroes, mistakes that teach, gray areas
**Themes:** Learning from failure, changing your mind, unintended consequences
**Example:** "The explorer who was brave but wrong—and what he learned"

**Integration with Sol:**
- Ancient Worlds → Geography (their routes, maps)
- Inventors → Weather, seasons affecting their work
- Character → Food chains, ecosystems (interdependence)
- Fairness → Different cultures, world geography

### August-October 2026: 3rd Grade Intensive (Nuanced Characters)
**Story Style:** Complex motivations, no clear villains, hard choices
**Themes:** Justice vs. mercy, individual vs. group, short vs. long term
**Example:** "The leader who had to choose between two good things"

**Integration with Sol:**
- Ancient Worlds → Engineering feats (pyramids, aqueducts)
- Inventors → Matter/energy, scientific method
- Character → Force/motion, physical challenges
- Fairness → Government branches, rights/responsibilities

### November-December 2026: Mastery (Philosophical Questions)
**Story Style:** Situations with no right answer, philosophical dilemmas
**Themes:** What is justice? Is it ever right to break rules? What makes a good life?
**Example:** "The person who broke a rule to help someone—was it right?"

**Integration with Sol:**
- Ancient Worlds → Legacy, historical change
- Inventors → Modern technology ethics
- Character → Biomechanics, peak performance
- Fairness → Civics, citizenship, democracy

## Pedagogical Approach: The Story Arc

Every story follows this structure:

```
🏛️ [Title: Character + Challenge]

[Setting: Time/place with one vivid detail]

[Character: Name, age, one quirk, what they want]

[The Challenge: What went wrong? Stakes?]

[The Choice: What did they do? Why was it hard?]

[The Outcome: What happened—success, failure, or both?]

💭 The Wisdom: [Clear lesson tied to complexity level]

🤔 Your Turn: [Question at Evaan's current level]

💬 Dinner Discussion: [Family conversation prompt]
```

- Question: What's more important—being right or doing right?

- Philosophical: Lied constantly, but for good reasons
- Question: Can a good person lie? What makes an action right?

## Content Guidelines

### Story Principles
1. **Start in the middle:** "The day everything changed..."
2. **One main character** — name, age, one memorable detail
3. **Concrete specifics:** "Her shoes had holes" not "She was poor"
4. **Show the choice:** What did they want? What blocked them?
5. **Make it hard:** The choice should have costs
6. **Connect to Evaan:** "You might face this when..."

### Length Guidelines
- **Minimum:** Complete arc (200 words)
- **Sweet spot:** 300-400 words
- **Maximum:** Stop when wisdom is clear
